<blockquote>

<p> We press our lips to the enameled rim of the cups</p>

<p>and know this grease that floats</p>

<p>over the coffee will one day stop our hearts.</p>

<p>Eyes and fingers drop onto silverware</p>

<p>that is not silverware. Outside the window, waves</p>

<p>beat against the chipped walls of the old city.</p>

<p>Your hands rise from the rough tablecloth</p>

<p>as if to prophesy. Your lips tremble...</p>

<p>I want to say to hell with the future.</p>

<p>Our future lies deep in the afternoon.</p>

<p>It is a narrow street with a cart and driver,</p>

<p>a driver who looks at us and hesitates,</p>

<p>then shakes his head. Meanwhile, </p>

<p>I coolly crack the egg of a fine Leghorn chicken.</p>

<p>Your eyes film. You turn from me and look across</p>

<p>the rooftops at the sea. Even the flies are still.</p>

<p>I crack the other egg.</p>

<p>Surely we have diminished one another.</p>

<figcaption>'Morning, Thinking of Empire' by Raymond Carver. Read May 2026</figcaption>
</blockquote>

I am not a poem person. I don't know what a Leghorn chicken is, and have not interest in finding out. I do not like the second half of the fifth and sixth stanza, I do not like the eight stanza. I had to look up the English word for stanza. 

I am a poem person when my heart is open and my head allows it. I drank filter coffee lest grease doesn't stop my heart. Until it became inconvenient and nespresso vertuo entered my house and I drank coffee in my new office where the machine makes only espresso. Surely we have diminished one another.
